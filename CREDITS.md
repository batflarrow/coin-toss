# Credits

Every bundled asset is public domain or CC0. None of them require attribution —
they are credited here anyway, and because knowing an asset's provenance is the
only way to stay confident it can be shipped.

All assets are regenerated from their sources by the scripts in `Tools/`, so
nothing here is a mystery binary.

The Five Pounds coin is an 1887 British gold £5: Boehm's Jubilee portrait of
Victoria on the obverse, Pistrucci's St George and the dragon on the reverse.

### Why only three currencies

These are the currencies for which matched obverse *and* reverse images exist
under a licence with no downstream obligations. Modern UK and Euro coin designs
are held by their issuing mints, and the freely-licensed photographs of them on
Wikimedia Commons are CC BY-SA — a share-alike term this project does not want
to take on. Adding another currency means finding a public-domain or CC0 pair
and appending it to the manifest in `Tools/PrepareCoinImages.swift`.

## Sounds

Rebuilt by `Tools/PrepareSounds.py`, which trims the useful moment out of each
recording, normalises it and applies short fades.

| Effect | Source | Licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `coin-toss.wav` | [Shaking coins in palm](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shaking_coins_in_palm.ogg) — ezwa, via pdsounds.org | Public domain |
| `coin-land.wav` | [Coin dropped on wooden floor](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Coin_dropped_on_wooden_floor.ogg) — ezwa, via pdsounds.org | Public domain |

Both are recordings of real coins. An earlier version synthesised these from
oscillators and it was immediately obvious they were fake.

## Drawn coins

Classic, Quarter, Doubloon, Bitcoin and Jade are drawn at runtime from SwiftUI
gradients and SF Symbols. No third-party assets are involved.
